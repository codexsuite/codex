Real-time collaboration: what the Plan and Write tabs will show

The UI reference for the product side of “Write it together”. Every mock below is drawn with the Codex tokens and the real Plan and Write markup, so what you see is what plan.html and write.html gain when the design task lands. Decisions were settled in review on 20 Oct 2026 and are listed in section 8.

What #26 adds

  • Header presence, hover card and Follow on both tabs
  • Write: live co-editing, remote carets, live gutter, anchored comments, offline merge
  • Plan: live board, card presence, co-edited summaries, conflicting-move snap

What is fixed already

  • Transport: Yjs over SignalR, server as a peer
  • Colour: a Knuth hash of the user id → hsl(h, 70%, 45%) (#15 §8)
  • Text goes through Yjs; board moves go through BoardChanged hub events (confirmed in this review, D6)

Blocked by

  • Sign-in (v1 runs as one seeded user)
  • Book membership: owner · co-author · reviewer
  • The v1 SignalR hub (#23 wave 3)

The design can land before any of these. The reference screens are static HTML.

Three people appear in every mock. Their colours are computed live on this page with the #15 §8 formula, from a hash of the user id, so they are the colours the app would really assign.

F-01 · One lightness does not survive both themes

At L 45% the blue, violet and red hues almost vanish against the dark surface, and a white label on a yellow hue is unreadable in light. The hash should pick the hue only; lightness and saturation come from the theme, like every other token. Suggest --user-l: 42% light, 62% dark, with the label colour flipping to --color-text on the light hues.

F-02 · User colours will collide with the category palette

Codex already uses five hues for meaning: bloglawstoryresearchperson plus accent blue and warn amber. A hashed hue will land on one of them for some users, so a caret can look like a Knowledge-base mention. The shapes differ (caret with a name tag, ring with an avatar), so this is tolerable. Decided (D3): accept the collision and document the shape rule; the hash keeps picking the hue.

2Both tabs: presence in the header

The bookbar gains one element: an avatar stack with a count, sitting just left of the save status. Nothing else moves. The reviewer’s own avatar stays at the far right where it is today.

  1. 1Avatar stack, newest joiner last, in each person’s colour. A green dot marks someone active in the last 30 s; an idle person fades to 45%. Four or more collapse to three avatars and +N. The label says 2 editing only while someone is active, otherwise 3 here. Reviewers who can only comment are in the stack too.
  2. 2Save status becomes sync status. With Yjs there is no “Unsaved”: every keystroke is applied locally and sent. The dot now reports the connection. States in section 5.

Follow

Same tab: scroll to the card or scene and flash it for 1.8 s, reusing the existing flash keyframe but in that person’s colour. Other tab: navigate with the existing deep links, write.html#scene=5.1 or plan.html#scene=…, then flash. Follow is a one-shot jump, not a lock: you are not pinned to their viewport afterwards.

Leaving

Closing the tab sends the awareness removal at once. A dropped connection is cleared by the server after 30 s. The issue also drops idle people at 30 s, which is too quick for a writer who stops to think. Decided (D5): fade at 30 s idle, remove at 5 min.

Narrow screens

Below 760px the bookbar already hides Scope, Format and Focus. The stack shrinks to one avatar with a count badge; the hover card becomes a sheet.

3Write tab: carets, selections, the live gutter and comments

One scene row from Chapter 5, with Priya typing in the second paragraph and a comment Marcus left on the first. The gutter keeps its sticky scene details; the open comment thread sits underneath them, in the same column, level with the passage it belongs to.

  1. 1Comment anchor. A warn-tinted highlight with a 2px underline, the same amber the References markers use for “needs check”, so it reads as “someone has a question here”. The open thread’s anchor is tinted stronger. Anchors are Yjs relative positions, so they move with the text.
  2. 2Remote selection at 22% of the person’s colour.
  3. 3Remote caret: a 2px bar with the first name in a tag above it. This is what @tiptap/extension-collaboration-caret renders; the styles above are the ones to hand it. The tag hides after 2 s of no movement and comes back on the next keystroke, so a parked caret does not cover prose.
  4. 4Live gutter. A small avatar in the scene header says who is in this scene. A field someone else is editing gets a 1.5px ring in their colour and a one-line caption; it drops out when their caret leaves. Summaries and labels are co-edited through Yjs, so the ring is the only “in use” signal, never a lock.
  5. 5Thread card, aligned to the passage’s first line (clamped so it never overlaps the sticky details). Quoted anchor, messages, Reply, Resolve. The toolbar shows 2 open; clicking it steps through open threads like the Codex highlight chip steps through scenes. Resolved threads leave the gutter and live under Resolved (n) in the scene ⋯ menu.

F-04 · Below 760px the gutter moves above the scene

That is where the thread card would go too, which pushes the prose down by a whole conversation. On narrow screens a tapped anchor should open the thread as a bottom sheet (the pattern the Knowledge-base panel already uses below 860px) and the gutter should show only the 2 open count.

F-05 · Focus mode

Focus hides everything but the prose. Remote carets should stay (you still need to see where a co-author is typing), comment anchors should fade to the underline only, and the header stack is hidden with the rest of the bookbar. Decided (D4): carets stay, name tags hidden.

F-06 · Reviewer role in Write

A reviewer’s prose is read-only: no caret of their own in the text, no / menu, Scene Beat buttons disabled. Selecting text still offers Comment. A quiet Reviewer · comment only chip in the Write toolbar explains why typing does nothing. Their presence avatar is drawn with a hollow ring instead of a fill so the stack shows who can edit.

F-07 · Comment entry point

Select text → a small floating Comment button at the selection end (Google Docs style), plus / → Comment and ⌘⌥M. The floating button is new chrome for Codex, but reviewers will not know the / menu. Decided (D2): all three routes.

4Plan tab: card presence, live moves and the losing move

Two chapter columns from Act 2. Marcus is editing the summary on Scene 1 of Chapter 7; Priya is dragging Scene 2 of Chapter 7 into Chapter 6, and the board shows her drop line to everyone.

  1. 1Card presence. A 1.5px ring in the person’s colour and a name tag on the top edge, for the card someone is editing. The caret inside the summary is the same remote caret as Write, without the name tag because the card already says who. The ring is thinner than the 2px accent rings the board uses for hit, drop-target and flash, and those win when both apply (F-03).
  2. 2A remote drag, where it started. The card being dragged dims to 55% where it sits and its tag says Priya is moving this. Only her own board shows the real dragged element.
  3. 3Where it will land. Her drop line and a ghost of the card appear in the target column, in her colour, so nobody is surprised when it jumps. On drop, the card slides into place (120 ms) and flashes once in her colour, the arrive variant of the existing flash. The same arrival flash marks a card someone else adds. Renames just change in place; the ring already says who.

When two people move the same scene

Board structure is not a CRDT: moves go through the command path and the server orders them. Both moves are applied in the order the server received them, the final order is broadcast, and the person whose move was overtaken sees their card snap to the server’s answer.

F-03 · State precedence on a card

A scene card can now carry five rings: dragging (35% opacity), drop-target (2px accent), hit (2px accent, from a Knowledge-base highlight), flash/arrive, and presence (1.5px in a colour). Rule: drag > drop-target > hit > arrive > presence, one ring at a time, and the presence name tag stays visible through all of them. Without this, a highlighted card that Marcus is editing would show two rings of different colours.

5Connection states, and what replaces the 409 banner

The bookbar dot today says Unsaved → Saving… → Saved. For a collaborative document it reports the link to the server instead. Edits never wait: they are applied locally and recovered by the reconnect handshake in #15 §4.

BookbarWhenWhat else the person sees
SyncedConnected and the last update acknowledged.Nothing.
Syncing…An update is in flight (normally under 100 ms, so this rarely shows).Nothing.
Reconnecting…The socket dropped; SignalR is retrying on its 0 / 250 / 1 s / 2 s / 5 s schedule.Remote carets freeze, then clear after 30 s. Editing continues.
Offline · edits keptRetries exhausted, or the browser reports no network.Header stack empties. A warn bar under the toolbar: You’re offline. Keep writing; your edits will merge when you’re back. No 409 conflict banner, ever, for Yjs-backed text.
SyncedBack online after an offline spell.Back online · merged 4 edits from Priya View

F-08 · Board moves while offline

Text merges, but a board move is a command and cannot be applied blind. While offline the grips are disabled with a tooltip Reordering needs a connection, and New scene / New chapter are queued and shown as ghost cards until acknowledged. Worth stating in #26, since “Offline” there only mentions prose.

6What travels how

Three lanes, which is why some things appear within 100 ms and some need a round trip.
